Prepared 2026-10-27. Scope: beat betting-market NBA winner predictions with an EQUINE head (any inputs), TimesFM 2.5 for stat lines, daily unattended runs on GitHub Actions free runners. Everything marked **verified** was probed on 2026-10-27 from the dev machine (the probe commands are reproduced in Section 6 so they can be re-run); everything else carries a citation. ## 1. Executive summary

**Is "beating the market" achievable, and against which line?**

- **Against a sharp closing line on sides: no public-data model has done it, and the project's own numbers reproduce the literature almost exactly.** Hubáček, Šourek & Železný's player-level CNN on 16,000 NBA games (2000–15) reached 68% accuracy vs Pinnacle's close at 70%, with a KL *disadvantage* of ≈ −0.015 nats/game and correlation 0.87 with the bookmaker before seeing odds ([IJF 2019](https://ida.fel.cvut.cz/papers/hubacek2019exploiting.html); [review Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827)); EQUINE is at 66.7% / Brier 0.208 vs DraftKings close 68.2% / 0.198 with correlation 0.90 and a stacking weight of −0.085. That 0.010 Brier gap is 3–5 standard errors on 1,061 games (per-game SD of the Brier difference ≈ 0.09–0.10 for a stand-alone model at this correlation; Monte-Carlo in Section 2 Q3), i.e. the model is *measurably worse* than the close, not noisily equal. Every documented NBA sides inefficiency with a clean test — home underdogs, back-to-backs, clinched seeds, star absences, reverse line movement, favorite–longshot bias — has been priced out of the close since roughly 2005–2012 (Section 2). Zimmermann's chance-based ceiling for NBA accuracy is 69.5–71.1% per season; the close sits at ≈70% ([Zimmermann](https://zimmermanna.users.greyc.fr/papers/conferences/chance-in-bball.pdf)).
- **Against the opening / morning line: yes, a measurable edge is supported by evidence, and it is the right target.** Opening lines contain significant errors when a meaningful player is absent and the market removes them by tip-off (Dare, Dennis & Paul 2015, 10,555 games, [FRL](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v13y2015icp130-136.html)); teams with a locked playoff seed show opening-line inefficiency at the 1% level but only weak closing-line inefficiency (Krieger et al. 2015, [FSR](https://openjournals.libs.uga.edu/fsr/article/download/3242/2876/9324)); open→close movement systematically improves forecast accuracy (Gandar et al. 1998, J. Finance). In the ESPN samples probed today the open→close moves are large (−190→−250, +188→+138). **The realistic claim is: match the close on sides (a strong result in itself), and beat the open/morning line by a few thousandths of Brier, mostly through injury-report timing and projected minutes.** Profit, if ever a goal, comes from *decorrelated residuals* (Hubáček & Šír 2023, ["Beating the market with a bad predictive model"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12508)) and CLV larger than the ≈4.5% NBA margin against a sharp reference (Whelan, 3,670 games 2022–25, [link](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)) — not from accuracy.

**Which line exists in the project's data (verified today, `sports.core.api.espn.com … /odds`):**

| Seasons | Close | Open | Multi-book consensus | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013-14 – 2016-17 | bare `moneyLine` of 5–9 offshore books (5Dimes, BetOnline, Bovada, SportsBetting.ag, Westgate, some Pinnacle 2015-17) | **yes** — a provider literally named `Opening` carries moneyline, spread, total | yes (books), but `consensus` provider has spread/total only | the opener in the sample differs from the books (−205 vs −190/−200) |
| 2017-18 – 2021-22 | bare `moneyLine` of 10–16 books (Caesars, Westgate, Wynn, Unibet, DraftKings from 2019-20, MGM, SugarHouse, PointsBet…) | **no** (no `Opening` provider, no `open` field) | yes; `consensus` now has a moneyline | bare `moneyLine` presumed = closing snapshot (verified only for 2022-23: ESPN BET `close` == bare `moneyLine` 6/6) |
| 2022-23 | as above (16 books) | ESPN BET `open` only (sparse) | yes | first season with `open/close/current` sub-objects |
| 2023-24 | 12–14 books, all with `close` | **yes**, every book has `open` | **yes** — best season for open + close + consensus | exclude "… - Live Odds" providers |
| 2024-25 | ESPN BET only | ESPN BET `open` | **no** (single book) | |
| 2025-26 | DraftKings only | DraftKings `open` | **no** (single soft book) | |

Consequences: (i) a decade-long backtest *vs the open* exists for 2013-17 and 2023-26, *vs the close* for every season, and a multi-book consensus for 2013-24 only; (ii) since 2024-25 the feed is a single soft book, and CLV against a soft-book close is weak evidence ([Buchdahl](https://football-data.co.uk/blog/nba_pinnacle_efficiency.php); Whelan) — a multi-book odds source (paid, e.g. The Odds API / OddsPapi; pricing unverified) will be needed to grade a live edge; (iii) nothing in the API has intraday timestamps, so the only way to ever own a timestamped "morning line" is to **log `current` at every run starting now**.

**Where the information can come from.** The official NBA injury report is the single most valuable unused source: 14,536 unique report PDFs (2018-19 → today, 205–243 game-days per season from 2019-20) are archived in the Wayback Machine with the publication time in the filename, and carry a free-text "Reason" column from the first archived report (21 Dec 2018) — verified today (Section 6.2). It fixes the project's known train/serve skew (hindsight availability in training vs ESPN "Out" at serve time) and is the backbone for projected minutes, for the text encoder, and for an honest open-vs-close evaluation. ESPN's own `summary` endpoint (already pulled) contains substitution-level play-by-play back to 2012-13, the three officials for every game, and the venue — enough for RAPM-style player impact, referee features and travel features without any new vendor.

**Project-level risk the owner must decide on (not resolved here).** The Disney Terms of Use that govern ESPN prohibit accessing or extracting Disney Products "using a robot, spider, script, or other automated means" and "for the avoidance of doubt, for the purposes of creating or developing any AI Tool, data mining or web scraping", and limit use to "personal, noncommercial" ([Sections 2.A, 2.B(x)](https://disneytermsofuse.com/english/); ESPN is named in Section 1). The whole pipeline is ESPN JSON. Options: (a) accept the risk for a personal, non-commercial, low-volume project (the endpoints are public, unauthenticated and widely used); (b) move history to `shufinskiy/nba_data` (Apache-2.0, stats.nba.com + pbpstats play-by-play 1996-97 → 2024-25, [repo](https://github.com/shufinskiy/nba_data)) and live data to NBA's own CDN (`cdn.nba.com/static/json/liveData`, cloud reachability unverified) plus the official injury PDFs and RSS feeds (ESPN, RealGM, CBS, HoopsRumors — published for syndication); (c) a paid feed. Sports-Reference's data-use terms explicitly ban using its data to train ML models ([link](https://www.sports-reference.com/data_use.html)), so Basketball-Reference is out regardless; api.pbpstats.com has no terms page that could be found (works, undocumented). Each experiment below carries its source's ToS status.

**Top-line recommendation (Phase 1, ≈ 25 h):** start logging lines and news at every run; build honest evaluation infrastructure (per-game persistence, additive/Shin/power de-vig, logit-mean consensus, open-vs-close gap, market-only baseline, HCA-by-era recalibration); build the injury-report archive and fix the availability skew. Only then build RAPM + projected minutes and the text encoder. JEPA is last; Jev-as-API is an appendix.

## 2. Evidence by question

### Q1. Efficiency of NBA lines; close vs open; realistic gains

- **Closing line quality.** The only public NBA closing-line Brier found is 0.203 (vigged bookmaker average, 2018-19 second half; Bradley–Terry 0.213–0.219, [R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2022/02/comparing-the-bradley-terry-model-to-betting-odds/)); TeamRankings 0.2167 vs 538 Elo 0.2179 on 2018-20 ([arXiv 2207.05114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05114)). The project's de-vigged DK close at 0.198 is consistent. The market–public-model gap is the usual 0.015–0.02 Brier.
- **Models vs close.** Hubáček 2019 (above); Kovalchik 2016 in tennis: a 7-book Shin-de-vigged logit-averaged consensus beat all 11 models on every metric ([JQAS](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/34652/)); Choe & Ramdas 2024 needed 25,165 MLB games to bound FiveThirtyEight's deficit vs Vegas at (−0.0027, −0.0006) Brier ([Ops. Res.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.00115)); Franck, Verbeek & Nüesch 2010: exchanges beat bookmakers in football ([IJF](https://unifr.ch/tim/en/assets/public/uploads/Publication%20list/2010/IJoF_Franck_Verbeek_Nuesch.pdf)). Walsh & Joshi 2024: select NBA models by calibration, not accuracy ([MLwA](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/machine-learning-for-sports-betting-should-model-selection-be-bas/)); Wunderlich & Memmert 2020: betting returns are not a valid accuracy measure ([IJF](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v36y2020i2p713-723.html)). Levitt 2004 is NFL-only ([EJ](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Levitt_Gambling_2004.pdf)).
- **Close vs open.** Gandar, Dare, Brown & Zuber 1998 (NBA spreads): line changes improve accuracy and remove opening bias by close. Dare, Dennis & Paul 2015: absences → opening errors, none at close. Krieger et al. 2015: clinched-seed inefficiency at open, weak at close. Practitioner corroboration: Pinnacle — "the first few bets taken on any overnight line are ALWAYS sharp plays" ([Doc's Sports](https://www.docsports.com/2006/pinnacle-pulse-32.html)); Circa opens NBA within a point of the offshore opener to avoid being "crushed" ([LSR](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/43632/betcris-bad-nba-betting-line/)); PFF (NFL): close picks the winner 65.9% vs 63.5% for the opener ([PFF](https://www.pff.com/news/bet-why-betting-early-critical-beating-nfl-markets)); Miller & Davidow: main lines are shaped by sharps, attack derivatives and copy-cat books ([LSR](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/33079/the-logic-of-sports-betting-market-agreement-and-resistance/)).
- **Known inefficiencies — status (sides unless noted):**
  - Favorite–longshot bias in NBA moneylines: *none*. Buchdahl, 13,401 Pinnacle NBA openers 2009-19: equal-margin de-vig closest to fair (−0.7%), proportional over-corrects (+1.2% theoretical yield) ([football-data](https://football-data.co.uk/blog/nba_pinnacle_efficiency.php)); Whelan 2022-25, ~10 US books: efficient, no FLB.
  - Home underdogs: profitable only for 10+ pt dogs in 2000-04, gone by 2004-08 ([Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/)).
  - Rest / back-to-back: Ashman, Bowman & Lambrinos 2010 — home on 2nd of B2B vs rested visitor covered 45.9% (1990–2009), worse after eastward travel ([JSE](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jospec/v11y2010i6p602-613.html)); Inpredictable 2003-10: market adjusted ~1.25 pts for no-rest vs ~2 realized, no extra for 2-in-3/3-in-4 ([link](https://www.inpredictable.com/2012/02/nba-home-court-advantage-and-rest.html)); since 2005 fading B2B teams is 50.3% ATS ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/moore-dont-be-fooled-by-blindly-fading-teams-on-back-to-backs)) → priced; a small residual may remain in the narrow Ashman spot.
  - Early season: Baryla, Borghesi, Dare & Dennis 2007 — *totals* biased (56.7% vs the close), sides not ([FRL](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v4y2007i3p155-164.html)).
  - Public/sentiment: Paul & Weinbach 2008 — fading the public does not win in the NBA ([IJSF](https://ideas.repec.org/a/jsf/intjsf/v3y2008i3p137-145.html)); Feddersen, Humphreys & Soebbing 2018 — spreads shaded toward popular home teams with no bettor return ([JSE](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jospec/v19y2018i4p455-472.html)).
  - Tanking/motivation: markets "believe" tanking and price it ([U. Alberta WP](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~econwps/2010/wp2010-13.pdf)); clinched-seed resting is now overt and public before tip.
  - Referees: Price & Wolfers own-race bias beat the spread historically ([BYU](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/192)); Pelechrinis 2023 (L2M reports 2015+): home-team bias shrinking since COVID ([Sci. Rep.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10031197/)); no crew→line study exists.
  - Reverse line movement / steam: 49.9% ATS since 2005 ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/betting-reverse-line-movement-nba-playoffs)); opening favorites 50.0% ATS over 20k games ([TeamRankings](https://betiq.teamrankings.com/nba/betting-trends/ats-results-by-opening-line-and-line-movement/)).
  - Injury-news timing: the one documented edge is open→close (Dare et al.); no academic event study of repricing *speed* exists.
- **Realistic magnitudes.** Stand-alone model vs close: parity is the ceiling. Stacked model vs the market-only baseline: +0.001–0.003 Brier plausible from injury timing/minutes, concentrated in games with Questionable/Probable starters; vs the open: larger (the open→close gap itself, to be measured in E2, is the prize size).

### Q2. Strongest public models and obtainable inputs

- **Models.** DARKO: decay-weighted box projections + Kalman + GBDT, DPM, "Roster Lab" team strength = Σ DPM × minutes share; minutes called "the hardest thing to project"; CSV download buttons, gating unverified ([about](https://www.darko.app/about)). EPM: SPM priors → decay-weighted ridge RAPM; live 2024-26 record 68.4% winners vs 67.5% for an opponent-adjusted net-rating baseline, backtest 2005-24 67.8% vs 66.3%; minutes model beats season-to-date by 14.5%; API $250/yr ([about](https://dunksandthrees.com/about/epm)). LEBRON: box prior + luck-adjusted RAPM ([BBI](https://www.bball-index.com/lebron-introduction/)). 538 RAPTOR/PREDATOR: free static CSVs through 2022-23 ([GitHub](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/nba-raptor), verified 200); the game model used depth-chart minutes (60% recent / 40% depth chart on game day) and blended minutes-weighted PREDATOR with Elo 0–55% by roster continuity, HCA ≈ 70 Elo, B2B −46, travel and altitude terms (Wayback copy of the 538 methodology page). BPM 2.0 is box-only and Sports-Reference bans ML use. xRAPM/RPM: no download. Inpredictable fits ratings to past spreads (market-derived by construction, [methodology](https://www.inpredictable.com/p/methodology.html)). No published head-to-head of any of these vs the close.
- **Player-level availability gains (the one input class with measured value):** Osken & Onay 2022 — 70.65% with player-cluster minutes vs 67.34% team-only on a clean 2017-18 holdout ([Heliyon](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9764182/)); 64NDA/NBA_Match_Predictor (open source, closest precedent to this project: PBP → stints → ridge RAPM over 423k stints → LightGBM P(plays) from the official report 60 min pre-tip → minutes redistributed to 240 → Σ rating × minutes + Elo/HCA/rest/travel) — walk-forward 2021-25 log-loss 0.606 vs Elo 0.627 (67.1% vs 64.8%), 2025-26 0.591 vs 0.604 ([repo](https://github.com/64NDA/NBA_Match_Predictor), self-reported, no license). Hubáček's player-level model gained ~0.2 pts over team-level.
- **Data sources (ToS status in brackets):**
  - ESPN `summary` [Disney ToU risk]: `plays` with `Substitution` events (entering/leaving athlete ids, team, period, clock, wallclock) back to 2012-13 — verified on 2014, 2017, 2020, 2024 games; `boxscore.athletes[].starter`; `gameInfo.officials` (3 referees, every season); `gameInfo.venue`; `winprobability[0]` (ESPN pre-game probability, present from ~2020, absent 2014/2017); `injuries`. Caveat found by probing: 2013-era PBP does not log between-period lineup changes (10/40 subs referenced an untracked player), so period-start lineups must be inferred from first actions, and some 2013 box minutes are blank — validate per game.
  - api.pbpstats.com [no ToS found; undocumented FastAPI with `/docs` and `/openapi.json`, verified]: `get-totals` Type=Lineup/Player/Team back to 2001-02, `get-games`, `get-wowy-stats`, `get-on-off`, `get-game-logs`; `get-possessions` capped at 500 rows with no lineup fields → not a bulk stint source. Cloud reachability unverified.
  - shufinskiy/nba_data [Apache-2.0]: bulk PBP + shot detail 1996-97 → 2024-25, pbpstats-derived PBP with possession starts 2000-01 →; last update Feb 2025 → history only.
  - stats.nba.com / nba_api [blocked from GitHub Actions, AWS, GCP, Azure; proxies work] ([issue #320](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/issues/320)); times out from the dev machine too.
  - Official injury PDFs [public league documents]: live PDFs are retained for roughly the current season plus ~9 months (verified: 2026-04-10 and 2026-01-15 → 200; 2025-11-20 and older → 403); everything older is Wayback-only (14.5k PDFs 2018-19 →); StatSurge static CSV Oct 2021–Jun 2024 ([link](https://statsurge.substack.com/p/downloadable-nba-injury-datasets)); parsers `nba-injury-report-pdf-to-df` (MIT), `nba-injury-report` (AGPL). Policy: day-before report by 5 pm local, game-day 1:30 pm ET, updated continually.
  - Referees: `gameInfo.officials` suffices; official.nba.com has today only (Wayback 956 days 2015-26).
  - Arenas: geocode the ~30 venues once by hand (lat/lon, elevation, time zone) from `gameInfo.venue`; no maintained free dataset; Positive Residual is dead.
  - Kaggle: wyattowalsh/basketball (nba_api-built SQLite incl. PlayByPlayV2, MIT tooling); no stint dataset found.
  - News text [RSS is the sanctioned route]: ESPN NBA RSS, RealGM Wiretap (injury-dense), CBS, HoopsRumors; ESPN `/news` and `/injuries` JSON have **no Wayback captures** → forward-log only.
- **RAPM recipe.** Stints from starters + substitutions; rows = stints (weight = possessions), columns = ±1 player indicators (or separate O/D columns), target = margin/100 poss with HCA intercept; ridge λ by CV; box-score prior (SPM regressed on multi-year RAPM, or DSMok1's regressed-MPG/award prior, r = 0.87 off / 0.59 def vs 3-yr RAPM, [APBR](https://www.apbr.org/metrics/viewtopic.php?p=41210)) via residual-ridge or pseudo-rows; 3–4 seasons stacked with decay; luck adjustment of opponent 3P%/FT% ([squared2020 tutorial](https://squared2020.com/2017/09/18/deep-dive-on-regularized-adjusted-plus-minus-ii-basic-application-to-2017-nba-data-with-r/); [Royce Webb](https://www.roycewebb.com/p/how-to-build-an-nba-box-score-metric); hoopR `nba_rapm` for reference). Compute: 300k stints × 550 players sparse ridge < 2 s; stint reconstruction is I/O-bound on cached JSON — trivially inside the runner budget.
- **Projected minutes.** 538/DARKO/EPM/DFS practice converge on: baseline = blend of recent (last 5–15 days) and season/depth-chart minutes; remove Out/Doubtful (and P(plays) for Questionable/Probable, learned from the report history); redistribute vacated minutes to same-position/next-in-rotation players; rescale to 240 (48×5); expected team strength = Σ rating × E[minutes]/240; add variance of strength from P(plays) uncertainty as a feature (EQUINE's GP variance will also react to it).

### Q3. Market-as-input architecture, de-vig, consensus, honest evaluation

- **De-vig.** Proportional (current) over-inflates NBA underdogs by ≈1 pp (Buchdahl, above). Štrumbelj 2014: Shin beats multiplicative by ranked probability score (via [Kovalchik](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/34652/), [arXiv 1802.08848](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08848)); Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram 2017: the **power** method "universally outperforms the multiplicative method and outperforms or is comparable to Shin" ([AJSS](https://www.sciencepublishinggroup.com/article/10.11648/j.ajss.20170506.12)); for two outcomes additive ≡ Shin. Use additive/Shin or power (fit exponent on 2013-24), keep proportional as an ablation. Second-order for Brier, first-order for CLV grading. Implementations: PyPI `shin`, R `implied`.
- **Consensus.** De-vig each book, drop model providers (numberfire, teamrankings, accuscore) and live-odds feeds, dedupe identical feeds (Caesars state clones), then **mean on the logit scale** (Leitner–Zeileis–Hornik; Kovalchik eq. 6) or median (Whelan). Board-size study: 3 well-chosen books are within 0.2–0.35 pp of a 176-book board; bet365-heavy sets are off by 1.3 pp; ≈25 books recommended for CLV grading ([OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=3710)). Pinnacle is the reference when available (2015-17 in ESPN data), not an average of soft books ([Buchdahl](https://football-data.co.uk/blog/pinnacle_wisdom.php)).
- **Combining market and model.** Three designs, all compatible with the EQUINE constraint: (A) *market logit as the 26th feature* of the embedding MLP (simplest; GP variance then means "familiarity of this market + feature configuration" — still a gating signal, different meaning); (B) *residual modeling*: EquineGP predicts P(home) with the market logit entered as a fixed offset (logit(p) = logit(m) + f(x)), so the embedding only has to learn the market's error — closest to Hubáček & Šír's prescription; (C) *two-stage stacking*: EquineGP(features) and the market logit combined by a walk-forward logistic stacker (the current test). (B) is recommended as the primary; (A) as the ablation. Add *open* logit and (open→current) delta as features when available — the delta encodes day-of-game news.
- **The control, defined precisely.** "Beat the market" is unfalsifiable once the market is an input unless the baseline is **a model fit on the market logit alone** (EquineGP or logistic, walk-forward). Report two deltas: raw de-vigged price vs market-only model (de-vig/calibration error) and market-only model vs full model (incremental information).
- **Statistics.** Per-game paired differences in Brier and log-loss; for independent games the Diebold–Mariano test reduces to a paired t-test (Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold correction for small n); paired bootstrap CIs; for a *live* log that is peeked at daily, use anytime-valid confidence sequences / e-processes (Choe & Ramdas, package `comparecast`) instead of a fixed-n test. Power (Monte-Carlo with closing-line home logit ~ N(0.25, 1), true probability = market logit + s·z for hidden information z, outcomes Bernoulli; consistent with an independent simulation by the market-research agent): **stand-alone** challenger at corr ≈ 0.9 → per-game SD of the Brier difference ≈ 0.09–0.10 → a 0.003 Brier edge needs ≈ 7,000 games (6 seasons) for 80% power at α = 0.05, 0.005 ≈ 2,500, 0.010 ≈ 650; **stacked** challenger that moves little off the market → SD ≈ 0.025–0.05 → 0.003 needs ≈ 900–2,000 games (1–1.6 seasons), 0.005 ≈ 450–1,000, 0.001 ≈ 2,500–6,000. Almost every experiment below is a stacked comparison. Prerequisite: `backtest` must persist per-game rows (p_model, p_market_open, p_market_close, outcome) — today `site/data/backtest.json` has aggregates only and `data/predictions` has 19 rows, so the empirical SD cannot be computed. Never evaluate on ROI (SE ≈ 2.9 pp at 1,200 flat bets); if ever betting, quarter-Kelly or less — full Kelly ruined 100% of runs even for Hubáček's decorrelated model ([review Table 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827)).
- **Schedule fix.** The 21:30 UTC run is 5:30 pm EDT but 4:30 pm EST, and the official report lands ≈5:30 pm ET; add a run ≈20 min after the report (and after the 1:30 pm ET game-day update) and log `current` odds at every run — the 10:15 UTC snapshot *is* a morning line for that night's games.

### Q4. LLMs (hosted and open-weight) — summary; the owner's "System One" design is Section 3

- **LLM-as-forecaster never beats the close.** Halawi et al. 2024: sports Brier 0.175 vs crowd 0.171 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563)); Schoenegger et al. "Silicon Crowd": 12-LLM ensemble ≈ human crowd, acquiescence bias ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379v6)); LLM-SoccerArena 2026: best of 7 frontier LLMs vs de-vigged close +0.001 Brier, CI [−0.006, 0.008], Holm p = 1.0 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24573)); Prophet Arena (ICLR 2026, 76% sports): GPT-5 0.184 vs market 0.187, no model break-even, sports the hardest category, market adjusts faster near resolution ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2)). Leakage pitfalls: Paleka et al. 2025 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00723)).
- **Extraction** works (GPT on ACL news: sensitivity 88.4%, specificity 99.3%, Diniz et al. 2025, KSSTA, doi 10.1002/ksa.12571) but the official report already structures status + reason; the marginal text is minutes restrictions / ramp-ups / "game-time decision", which exists only in forward-logged prose.
- **Hosted options (official pages, 2026-10-27):** GitHub Models retired 30 Jul 2026 ([changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-30-github-models-is-now-retired/)); Groq free plan 30 RPM / 1k RPD / 8k TPM / 200k TPD ([docs](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)); Gemini free tier exists but limits are unpublished and prompts are used for training ([pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), [terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)); OpenRouter `:free` 20 RPM, 50 RPD (<$10 lifetime) / 1,000 RPD (≥$10) ([limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits)); Cerebras now a $5/30-day trial; HF Inference Providers $0 free credit; Together/Fireworks ≈ $0.10–0.17 per M tokens → ≈ $0.20/month for 100 notes/day. Compute is not the constraint; information is.
- **Local CPU:** Qwen2.5-3B Q4 on 4 threads: pp512 43 t/s, tg 11.8 t/s ([anchor](https://computingforgeeks.com/run-local-llm-llama-cpp/)); 1–1.7B models ≈ 2–3× faster; 100 short notes/day ≈ 5–7 min for a 1B model with generation, ≈ 1–3 min for embedding-only. GGUF 0.7 GB (1B) fits `actions/cache` (10 GB, 7-day eviction; the daily cron keeps it warm).

### Q5. JEPA / self-supervised sports representations — verdict: last

- JEPA family: I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2 (multi-block masking, EMA target encoder, latent L2); TS-JEPA for time series: frozen encoder *trails* supervised on 4/5 datasets of 3.6k–11k samples at full labels and only wins at 5–20% labels ([arXiv 2509.25449](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25449v1)); T-JEPA for tabular (ICLR 2025): gains on Adult/Jannis/Helena, **collapse on Higgs (0.68 → 0.52)**, EMA insufficient — needed a never-masked [REG] token; beats XGBoost on 3/7 ([arXiv 2410.05016](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05016v3)). Rubachev et al. 2022: target-aware pretraining objectives best ([arXiv 2207.03208](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.03208)); Grinsztajn 2022 and McElfresh 2023: trees remain SOTA at ~10k rows; TabPFN v2 (Nature 2025) is the strong small-data baseline ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39780007/)). LeJEPA (Balestriero & LeCun, Nov 2025): SIGReg replaces EMA/stop-grad with an isotropic-Gaussian embedding regularizer in ~50 lines ([arXiv 2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544), [code](https://github.com/rbalestr-lab/lejepa)) — the one variant worth trying here, because an isotropic-Gaussian embedding is exactly what EquineGP's stationary RFF kernel assumes.
- Sports: baller2vec++/CourtMotion/TacticExpert need tracking data; NBA2Vec (8-d player embeddings from 3.5M plays, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.13386)) and LinNet (lineup node2vec, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01855v2)) report no game-outcome or Vegas benchmark; GCN+RF 71.5% and LSTM 72.4% papers have unverifiable splits; Migliorati 2021: Elo alone beats Four-Factors features ([arXiv 2111.09695](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09695v1)). TSFM embeddings (MOMENT, Mantis, Chronos/TimesFM) help on long regular UCR-style series; StarEmbed shows FM embeddings *below* hand-crafted features on irregular signals ([arXiv 2510.06200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06200)); nothing on short noisy game logs.
- **Candid expected value.** All 19k games are labeled; the market logit summarizes most public information; the closest precedent (Hubáček's deep model over player rows) was 0.87-correlated with the bookmaker before seeing odds. A JEPA encoder is likely to improve the GP head's OOD/calibration behaviour more than its discrimination vs the close. If built: LeJEPA/SIGReg over per-game team sequences (last 20 games × [four factors, pace, rotation minutes, availability, rest]) with the *target* being next-game latent, evaluated only as a stacked input conditional on the market logit, after TabPFN v2 / CatBoost on the same 25 features have been run as the "is deep learning even needed" control. Effort 30–40 h; prior on gain ≤ 0.001 Brier vs the stacked baseline.

## 3. Building an open "System One" model: LLM-encoder → EQUINE

The working design (serialize structured pre-game state + pre-tip text → small open-weight LLM as a single-pass encoder → EquineGP on [LLM embedding ⊕ engineered features ⊕ market logit] → calibrated typed outputs) is sound as an *architecture*; EQUINE already does the "typed, calibrated output" half of what Jev sells (a Laplace GP over RFF with temperature scaling, plus a variance that Jev's `confidence` field does not have — Jev's confidence is a deterministic function of the returned distribution, [docs](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence)). What the literature says about the *encoder* half is more sobering, so the section below is organized as: evidence → backbone choice → leakage control → training objective (SFT vs RL) → historical text availability → minimal experiment.

### (d) Does an LLM encoder add signal over strong engineered features? Evidence

- **Tabular with text columns.** The most systematic study (Kolomenko, Knauer & Rodner 2026, 256 pipeline configurations, 16 embedding models) finds LLM embeddings help *conditionally*: concatenating embeddings with the original columns beats replacing them, larger embedding models do better, GBDTs remain strong downstream learners, and leaderboard rank is a poor predictor of tabular gains ([arXiv 2603.17737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17737)). The CARTE/ConTextTab line reports gains in the *low-data, semantically rich* regime (LightGBM 73.4% vs ConTextTab 76.9% vs AutoGluon 78.7% on CARTE classification) and a follow-up shows the gain vanishes when semantic content is removed ([ConTextTab, NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/d807e7678ba3afd3a904f4af52819e77-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv 2608.03565](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03565v1)). A 2025 benchmark of foundation models for tabular-with-text notes LLM-based predictors "do not perform well" in the many-shot regime ([arXiv 2507.07829](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07829)). TabLLM's advantage is a few-shot phenomenon ([Hegselmann et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/hegselmann23a/hegselmann23a.pdf)).
- **Sports text specifically.** The one rigorous study of text-for-sports-prediction (Oved, Feder & Reichart 2019: pre-game interview language predicting in-game deviations) finds text beats metric-only baselines and text+metrics is best — but the target is player behaviour, not game outcomes ([arXiv 1910.11292](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11292)). No published work was found that uses injury-report or news embeddings to improve NBA/NFL game-outcome prediction vs a market line.
- **Decoder LLM as encoder.** Raw last-token/mean-pooled hidden states from a causal LM are mediocre embeddings; LLM2Vec (bidirectional attention + masked-next-token + unsupervised contrastive adaptation) is what makes a decoder a good encoder, and its smallest tested model is 1.3B ([arXiv 2404.05961](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05961v1)). For a 0.5–1.7B backbone, the practical recipe is therefore: LoRA-tune the backbone *end-to-end with the EQUINE head* (so the pooled state is shaped by the task), or use a purpose-built small embedding model (bge/gte/e5-small, 33–110M params, 2023 cutoffs) for the text and keep the LLM out of the inference path entirely.
- **Implication for this project.** ~19k games with 25 well-engineered numeric features is the *many-shot, numeric* regime where LLM encoders are weakest; the market logit already summarizes most public information. The only channel where an encoder can plausibly add signal is the **free text that the structured features cannot see**: the "Reason" column of the official injury report (e.g. "Return to Competition Reconditioning", "Injury Management", "Left Knee; Surgery" vs "Illness"), ESPN/Rotowire injury blurbs (minutes restrictions, "ramp-up", "game-time decision"), and coach quotes. That is a *player-availability/minutes* signal, which the current roster-availability feature only sees as Out/not-Out. Expected value therefore depends on how much minutes-restriction information the market has NOT absorbed by the time of the run — the literature on injury-news timing (Section 2, Q1) suggests most is absorbed within minutes of the 5:30 pm ET report. Honest prior: a small (≤0.002 Brier) gain vs the close, concentrated in games where a starter is Questionable/Probable or returning from a long absence; a larger gain vs the *opening* line.

Qwen2.5-0.5B halves this. The machine's current load average (13 on 8 cores) means training must run overnight.

### (b) Leakage control

1. **Backbone cutoff.** Pick by documented cutoff (Llama 3.2 = Dec 2023; Gemma 3 = Aug 2024). Test seasons 2024-25 and 2025-26 are strictly after both. Treat Qwen2.5/SmolLM2 as "probably clean, undocumented"; never report a Qwen3-based number on 2024-25.
2. **Anonymize the structured part.** Replace team names with role tokens (HOME/AWAY) and player names with stable per-game pseudonyms (P1…P15 ordered by projected minutes) in the *structured* serialization, so the model cannot retrieve memorized results or memorized player quality; keep the numeric features as text numbers (rounded) and let the engineered features carry team identity to EQUINE separately. Keep real player names only inside the *text* fields if the experiment is explicitly testing whether world knowledge about a player helps (that arm is then not leakage-safe for seasons before the cutoff — and for Llama 3.2 it is safe only for 2024-25+).
3. **Text timestamps.** Use only text whose timestamp (injury-report PDF filename time, ESPN `published`) precedes the prediction time; the archived report nearest to but before the 5:30 pm ET run is the canonical snapshot for both training and serving (this also fixes the existing availability train/serve skew).
4. **Validation.** Walk-forward by season, with pre-registered metrics; a within-cutoff "canary" (train on 2022-23, test on 2023-24 with real names) vs the same with anonymized names tells you how much memorization leaks in for seasons before the cutoff.

### Training objective: supervised proper score vs RL ("RLCD" analogue)

- **Case 1 — the policy samples a class token (home/away) and is rewarded on it.** Expected reward E_a~π[r(a, y)] is *linear* in π(a|x), so its maximum over the simplex is a vertex: the optimum is argmax, and the token probabilities are driven toward 0/1 no matter what the per-sample reward is called (−Brier of a one-hot sample is just a rescaled 0/1 correctness reward). This is miscalibration by construction — exactly the "ordinary RL hurts calibration" finding of Damani et al. ([RLCR, ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16806)). GRPO on a categorical choice token is therefore the wrong tool for a calibrated forecaster.
- **Case 2 — the action is itself a verbalized probability** (Turtel et al.: the model emits "0.63"; Damani et al.: answer + confidence). Now −Brier of the emitted number is a proper score and the optimum *is* calibrated. But a policy gradient on a sampled number is a high-variance Monte-Carlo estimate of the gradient that a differentiable head computes exactly: minimizing log-loss/Brier of a softmax (or EquineGP) output on the same data optimizes the same objective with no sampling noise, no KL/clip hyper-parameters and no reward-hacking guardrails. For a single-step output, RL "for calibrated decisions" is a slower, noisier way to do what the EQUINE/log-loss head already does.
- **Case 3 — a free-form reasoning trace precedes the output.** Here the trace cannot be supervised (no gold rationales), the probability is a function of sampled tokens, and RL earns its keep. This is Turtel et al.'s setting and the only one where "RL for calibrated decisions" is more than a label.
- Evidence for Case 3: Turtel et al. train a 14B reasoning model with GRPO/ReMax on −Brier over 10k Polymarket questions and reach Brier 0.190 (o1: 0.202; base R1-14B: 0.215) but **still lose to the market (0.151)** by 0.039, and needed guardrails against reward-hacking collapse into 0/100% predictions (39% of predictions in the extreme buckets without the fix — Case 1 behaviour leaking in) ([arXiv 2505.17989](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17989v4)). That paper does not compare RL to SFT, and its compute (8×H100 × 3 days) is out of scope here.
- **Recommendation:** train the LoRA + EQUINE head jointly (or in two stages: LoRA with a linear log-loss head, then freeze and fit EquineGP on the pooled embedding ⊕ features ⊕ market logit) with **log-loss on the outcome**, plus EQUINE's existing temperature scaling. Use Brier as the *selection* metric. Add auxiliary heads (per-player played/not, minutes bucket) as multi-task log-loss terms — these are the "typed outputs" and they give the encoder a dense supervised signal that the single win/loss bit cannot. Skip GRPO; if a reasoning-trace variant is ever wanted, it should be a separate, hosted-model experiment judged only in shadow mode.

### (c) Does historical pre-game text exist? (verified 2026-10-27)

- **official.nba.com season pages:** `nba-injury-report-2023-24-season/`, `…-2024-25-season/` etc. all **301-redirect to the current (2026-27) page**; only the current season's index is served. Direct PDF URLs on `ak-static.cms.nba.com/referee/injury/` return 200 for roughly the current season plus ~9 months (2026-04-10 and 2026-01-15 → 200) and **403 for older dates** (2025-11-20 and earlier; removed from the CDN).
- **Wayback Machine** has archived **14,536 unique injury-report PDFs**: 2018-19: 104 game-days, 2019-20: 205, 2020-21: 206, 2021-22: 243, 2022-23: 227, 2023-24: 224, 2024-25: 228, 2025-26: 220 (exact CDX query in Section 6.2). Each filename carries the report timestamp, so alignment to tip-off is exact. This is the single most valuable text+structured archive for the project.
- **Reason column:** present from the earliest archived report. The 21 Dec 2018 report has separate `Category` ("Injury/Illness", "G League Team", "Not With Team") and `Reason` ("Left Wrist Surgery") columns; from 2019-20 the two merge into one `Reason` field in the form "Injury/Illness - Right Knee Soreness", and from January 2020 the standardized "Injury/Illness - <Body part>; <Detail>" format is used (e.g. "Injury/Illness - Left Lumbar Spine; Stress Reaction", "Return to Competition Reconditioning", "Health and Safety Protocols", "Coach's Decision - …", "G League - Two-Way", "Not With Team", "Injury/Illness - N/a; Surgical Recovery"). Statuses Out/Doubtful/Questionable/Probable/Available appear throughout; "NOT YET SUBMITTED" rows mark teams that have not filed. The text is terse (≈6–10 tokens/row) — enough for a learned embedding of injury *type/severity*, not for minutes-restriction nuance.
- **Other time-stamped pre-game text:** ESPN `site.api.espn.com/…/news` and `/injuries` (`longComment` Rotowire-style prose) are current-only with no archive (no Wayback captures of the JSON endpoints; espn.com/nba/injuries HTML has ~6k snapshots 2016–2026, unparsed). Rotowire/CBS news archives are paywalled or ToS-restricted. **Practical plan:** start logging ESPN injuries `longComment` + RSS news at every daily run now (it is the only way to get a clean history), and train the encoder initially on the official-report rows only.

### (e) Minimal first experiment (and cost)

**Experiment S0 — infrastructure (≈8 h; this is E1 of Section 4):** Wayback bulk-download of the 14.5k PDFs (rate-limit politely; ~0.4 GB), parse with `pypdf` layout text → table (team, player, status, reason, report timestamp), choose per game the last report before 5:30 pm ET on game day (and the 1:30 pm one as a "morning" variant); join to ESPN player ids by name+team. Deliverable: `data/injury_reports/{season}.csv`. This is the same work as E1/E2 of Section 4.

**Experiment S1 — "is text even needed?" control (≈4 h, CPU only):** embed the concatenated injury-report rows per team with bge-small (384-d), mean-pool, PCA→16, append to the 25 features + market logit, refit EquineGP walk-forward on 2019-20→2025-26. Metric: paired Brier vs market-only EquineGP, pooled 2023-24..2025-26, paired bootstrap CI. If this shows nothing, the LLM encoder is very unlikely to.

**Experiment S2 — System One v0 (≈16–24 h wall, ~2–3 overnight runs on the M1):** Llama 3.2 1B (or Qwen2.5-0.5B), LoRA r=16, sequence = anonymized structured state (both teams' 25 features as rounded text, rest/B2B, market open & current logit) + injury-report rows for both teams; pooled last-token state (dim 2048) → linear projection 64 → trained with log-loss on home win **plus** auxiliary log-loss heads for each listed player's played/DNP (and minutes bucket) — multi-task gives ~10–20 labels/game instead of one. Train 2019-20..2022-23 (early-stop on 2023-24), evaluate 2024-25 and 2025-26 walk-forward (re-fit only the EquineGP head per season; LoRA frozen). Then fit EquineGP on [64-d projection ⊕ 25 features ⊕ market logit]. Compare three EQUINE heads: features+market (control), S1 (bge), S2 (LLM). Compute: embedding extraction for ~6k games ≈ 1 h on MPS; LoRA ≈ 2–4 h/epoch × 3 epochs. Runner cost: 15 games/day ≈ 3–10 min with the LoRA merged and exported to ONNX/int8 or GGUF (llama.cpp supports embedding extraction via `llama-embedding`).

**Expected result and decision rule.** Prior: S2 − control ∈ [−0.002, +0.003] Brier vs the close; a real improvement would most likely show up first as (i) better per-player played/minutes prediction (an auxiliary metric with 10× the sample size) and (ii) a positive stacking weight vs the market *open*. Promote S2 to the daily pipeline only if the pooled 2024-25+2025-26 paired-bootstrap 95% CI for Brier-vs-close excludes zero or the stacking weight on the open line is significant; otherwise keep S1/S2 as a logged shadow model. Cost of the whole S0–S2 chain: ~30–40 engineering hours, zero API spend, ~3 GB disk.

### Appendix to Section 3 — Jev / TypeSafe System One as a hosted API, and the shadow-log protocol

**What it is (vendor claims, no independent evaluation found).** TypeSafe's launch post describes Jev as a closed, early-access model that takes "state" (text and/or JSON) plus typed questions and returns, in one parallel pass, a typed answer with a probability distribution over the options: `Choice` (up to 255 options), `Score` (ordered rubric levels) and `Noul` (a 0–1 truth judgment). It is trained with an undisclosed "Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions" (RLCD); latency 70–500 ms; input $0.042/M tokens, output free; no weights, parameter count, paper or public benchmark. The only accuracy figure is TypeSafe's own four-workflow benchmark (~68% agreement with a reference that is the *average of two frontier LLMs*, not ground truth), and their own "193.6x faster / 444.6x cheaper" headline. ([launch post](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev), [docs overview](https://docs.typesafe.ai), [Confidence page](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence), [adapter repo](https://github.com/typesafe-ai/system-one-adapter-python)). The `confidence` field is a pure function of the returned distribution ((p_max − 1/n)/(1 − 1/n) for Choice), not an extra uncertainty signal. The Confidence page explicitly declines to report calibration and tells users to "test with your own data." Secondary write-ups ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/system-one-models-jev), [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-a-harness-with-jev), [Maio](https://anthonymaio.substack.com/p/jev-the-language-model-that-wont)) repeat the vendor numbers; one third-party model listing gives a 32k (jev-1.13) or 64k (jev-latest) context ([Maxim listing](https://www.getmaxim.ai/bifrost/model-library/compare/typesafe/jev-latest.md)); commentary reports that v1.13 is weak at arithmetic/counting, reads dates as text, and degrades as unrelated state grows ([systemonemodels.org RLCD explainer](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/rlcd-explained/)). An arXiv paper uses Jev as a decision layer for pentest agents but does not evaluate it ([arXiv 2609.28940](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28940)). No rate limits, data-retention or training-on-customer-data terms are published; early access is by waitlist. Verdict on evidence: **unverified calibration, unverified accuracy, closed weights, single vendor.**

**Use (1): calibrated extractor of availability / minutes outlook from text.** This is the use that fits Jev's design: short text state ("Injury/Illness – Left Knee; Soreness", an ESPN `longComment`, a beat-writer paragraph) → `Choice` over {Out, Doubtful, Questionable, Probable, Available} and `Score` over a minutes-outlook rubric {0, <15, 15–25, 25–32, full}, with probabilities EQUINE can consume as expected-minutes weights. Pros: cheap (a day's ~150 notes × ~200 tokens ≈ 30k tokens ≈ $0.001), fast, schema-guaranteed, trivially parallel. Cons: (a) the *statuses themselves are already structured* in the official injury report — Jev adds value only on the free text that modifies them (minutes restriction, "ramp-up", "game-time decision", return-from-long-absence), which is a minority of rows; (b) historical free text with timestamps is thin (ESPN injury comments are not archived; the official report "reason" column is archived via Wayback for 2018-19+ but is terse); (c) Jev's "calibration" is a vendor claim — treat its probabilities as *features*, let EQUINE's temperature/GP calibrate. Recommendation: worth a 4–8 h pilot **only after** the official-report pipeline (E1) exists, and benchmark it head-to-head against a local Qwen/Llama-class small model and a regex/keyword baseline on ~300 hand-labelled notes (κ agreement, Brier of P(plays) vs actual box-score minutes). The adapter repo makes the A/B trivial since it answers the same request shape with any LLM.

**Use (2): Jev as a direct per-game forecaster feeding EQUINE.** State = structured pre-game features + team/injury text; question = `Choice{home, away}` or `Score` over margin buckets; output probability becomes a 27th EQUINE input. Evidence against expecting much: every leakage-controlled study of LLM forecasters finds they are at best near, not above, aggregate/market forecasts, and their apparent backtest skill collapses when temporal leakage is removed ([Paleka et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00723); [Prophet Arena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17638); [HINDCAST](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2607.14051); [Evaluating LLMs vs expert forecasters](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04562)). Jev has no published forecasting result at all, its training data and cutoff are undisclosed, and its documented weakness at arithmetic/ordered quantities is exactly what a numeric game-strength comparison needs. Because its pretraining almost certainly contains historical NBA results and box scores, **no historical backtest of Jev is clean**; the only valid evaluation is a prospective shadow log.

**Shadow-log protocol (applies to Jev and any hosted LLM).**
1. At each daily run (ideally the post-report run), for every game that day, store: timestamp, request state hash, the hosted model's probabilities, EQUINE probability, the `current` moneyline from ESPN at that timestamp, and later the `close` and the result. Commit to `data/shadow/` like the other tables.
2. Primary metric: paired per-game Brier (and log-loss) difference vs the de-vigged close; secondary: vs the line *at prediction time*; also the logistic-stacking weight of the hosted model given the market logit (the current EQUINE test that returned −0.085).
3. Power: Monte-Carlo on NBA-like odds (logit ~ N(0.25, 1)) gives a per-game SD of the paired Brier difference of 0.03–0.10 depending on how far the challenger sits from the market. With one-sided α = 0.05 and 80% power: a 0.005 Brier gain needs ≈ 500–1,000 games (less than one season), a 0.003 gain ≈ 900–2,000 games (1–1.6 seasons), a 0.001 gain ≈ 2,500–6,000 games (2–5 seasons). A *blend* that moves only slightly off the market has lower variance, which is why detection is quicker for the stacked model than for a stand-alone forecaster. Because a daily log is inspected continuously, use anytime-valid inference — a confidence sequence / e-process on the per-game Brier differences (Choe & Ramdas; package `comparecast`) — rather than a fixed-n t-test; the fixed-n figures above are planning numbers only. Pre-register the metric, the reference line and the de-vig method.
4. Gate: a hosted-model stack only ships to the public page if the stacked model's Brier is better than the market-only model's in the shadow log at the 95% level; until then it is logged, not displayed.

**Bottom line on Jev.** It is a plausible, cheap *text-to-typed-feature* tool, not a forecaster. Its unique selling point (calibration) is unverified and, in this stack, redundant: EQUINE's GP + temperature already calibrate whatever comes in. Rank it below the official-injury-report pipeline (E1), the evaluation layer (E2) and projected-minutes work (E4); it is not a numbered experiment. Cost is negligible; the real cost is vendor dependence and the inability to backtest.

## 4. Ranked experiments

Conventions: walk-forward by season (train on all prior seasons; early-stop on the last 8% as today); **primary metric** = paired per-game Brier (and log-loss) difference vs the stated control, pooled over the stated test seasons, with a paired bootstrap 95% CI and a paired t-test (HLN-corrected); secondary = accuracy, ECE, stacking weight. "Market-only baseline" = EquineGP/logistic fit on the de-vigged market logit alone, walk-forward. Effort in engineering hours. ToS status of each data source is given in Section 2 Q2.

**E1. Official injury-report archive → fix the availability train/serve skew** (≈ 12 h; *prerequisite for E4–E7*)
Hypothesis: training on hindsight availability (box score) while serving on ESPN "Out" inflates every backtest and makes the market comparison apples-to-oranges; replacing both with the last official report before a fixed pre-tip cutoff makes evaluation honest and should *raise* live-equivalent quality relative to the serve-time feature while removing the train/serve gap.
Data: Wayback CDX + PDFs 2018-19 → (verified; public league documents), live PDFs for the current season (verified 200 with browser headers back to Jan 2026; Nov 2025 → 403), `pypdf` layout parsing (format verified for 2018, 2019, 2020-26); StatSurge CSV as a cross-check 2021-24.
Sketch: bulk-download ≈ 14.5k PDFs (≈ 0.4 GB) once on the dev machine, politely rate-limited; parse → `data/injury_reports/{season}.csv` (report_time, game, team, player, status, reason); join players to ESPN ids by name+team; for each game choose the last report before (a) 5:30 pm ET day-of and (b) 1:30 pm ET; rebuild the roster-availability feature with Out/Doubtful = 0, Questionable = P(plays) estimated from history (≈ 0.5–0.7), Probable ≈ 0.9; add counts by status and "star Questionable" flags. Daily job: fetch the newest PDF after each run.
Evaluation: 2019-20 → 2025-26 walk-forward, EQUINE Brier with new vs old availability; the gap between "hindsight" and "report-based" features quantified per season. Expected: honest numbers (possibly a worse backtest, a better live score), +0.002–0.005 Brier on games with a listed starter vs the current serve-time feature. Evidence: Osken & Onay +3.3 pts; 64NDA 0.606 vs 0.627.

**E2. Evaluation infrastructure: per-game persistence, de-vig, consensus, open-vs-close gap, market-only baseline, HCA-by-era** (≈ 10 h)
Hypothesis: the current comparison (one soft book, proportional de-vig, half a season) cannot detect the effect sizes that matter; the open→close Brier gap is the ceiling for all pre-close information.
Data: ESPN core odds (verified; Disney ToU risk) — `Opening` provider 2013-17, `open` fields 2023-26, books' bare `moneyLine` 2013-24, `consensus` ML 2017-24.
Sketch: persist per-game rows (p_model, p_open, p_close, p_consensus, outcome, novelty); implement proportional / additive-Shin / power de-vig; consensus = logit-mean of de-vigged books after dropping model providers, live-odds feeds and duplicate feeds; compute **Brier(open) − Brier(close) per season** (and verify that 2013-17 `Opening` differs from books' bare lines and that bare line ≈ close pre-2022 using 2022-23 as the anchor); fit the market-only baseline; recalibrate the Elo home constant per era (2012-19 vs 2020-21 vs 2021+). Report detectable effect sizes from the empirical SD.
Evaluation: all seasons; this experiment *produces* the evaluation. Expected: open→close gap of order 0.003–0.008 Brier in seasons with openers (prize size); de-vig change ≈ 1 pp on dogs; HCA fix ≈ +0.001 Brier for 2021+.

**E3. Line + news logging at every run, and a post-report run** (≈ 3 h; start immediately)
Hypothesis: no historical "morning line" exists anywhere; the project can own one. Data: ESPN `current` odds, ESPN `/injuries` `longComment`, RSS (ESPN, RealGM Wiretap, CBS, HoopsRumors — syndication feeds). Sketch: append `data/odds_log/`, `data/news_log/` at 10:15 UTC, 21:30 UTC and a new ≈ 22:00 UTC (22:50 in EST) run; store timestamps. Evaluation: none now; enables E4/E6 vs-morning-line tests after ≈ 1 season.

**E4. RAPM from ESPN play-by-play + projected minutes → expected team strength** (≈ 25 h)
Hypothesis: Σ player-impact × expected minutes, using the report-based availability from E1, carries information the market-only baseline lacks on days with multiple absences/returns, and more so vs the open.
Data: ESPN `summary.plays` substitutions + `boxscore.athletes[].starter` (verified 2014-26; Disney ToU risk) — or shufinskiy/nba_data PBP (Apache-2.0) for history; box-score prior from the project's own player-box table.
Sketch: stint reconstruction with period-start lineup inference (first on-court action per period) and per-game reconciliation against box minutes (target mean |err| < 0.5 min; a 2026 test game gave 0.26); 3-season decay-weighted ridge RAPM with box prior (residual ridge), refit weekly on the runner (<2 s solve; I/O from cache); minutes model: blend recent/season minutes, apply E1 availability, redistribute to 240, learn P(plays) for Questionable/Probable by logistic regression on report history; features for EQUINE: expected net strength, strength variance, Δ vs full-strength, top-3 absent-impact. Evaluation: 2019-20 → 2025-26, full model vs market-only baseline (primary) and vs features-without-E4 (ablation); subgroup: games with ≥1 rotation player Questionable/Doubtful. Expected: +0.002–0.004 Brier vs market-only on the subgroup, +0.001–0.002 overall; larger vs the open. Evidence: 64NDA, Osken & Onay, 538's minutes-weighted PREDATOR design, EPM live record.

**E5. Cheap schedule/circadian/motivation features** (≈ 6 h)
Hypothesis: travel direction × time zones × local tip hour, the Ashman interaction, schedule-density counts and clinched-seed/eliminated flags carry residual information vs the open and possibly the close. Data: own schedule + a hand-built 30-arena table (lat/lon, elevation, tz). Evaluation: vs market-only baseline, 2013-14 → 2025-26 (long history matters for small effects), stacked comparison; vs open on 2013-17 + 2023-26. Expected: ≤ 0.001 Brier vs close, more vs open; the circadian test is a publishable negative/positive either way.

**E6. "System One" text encoder — S1 control (bge-small embeddings of report text) then S2 (LoRA Llama 3.2 1B end-to-end with EquineGP)** (S1 ≈ 4 h; S0+S2 ≈ 30–40 h) — full design, backbones, leakage controls and cost in Section 3.
Evaluation: 2024-25 + 2025-26 pooled (strictly after the backbone cutoff), vs the market-only baseline and vs E1+E4 features without text; auxiliary metrics on per-player played/minutes buckets. Expected: S1 ≈ 0; S2 −0.002 to +0.003 Brier vs close, with the signal in Questionable/Probable-starter games.

**E7. Decorrelation-aware training of the EQUINE embedding** (≈ 8 h) — *a profit mechanism, not forecasting skill*.
Hypothesis (Hubáček & Šír): adding a penalty on the correlation between model and market probabilities (γ ≈ 0.4–0.6 in Hubáček 2019) yields a less accurate but decorrelated second opinion whose disagreements with the market are informative. Sketch: add a batch-wise correlation penalty to the EQUINE training loss; evaluate the *stacked* model (market + decorrelated EQUINE) vs market-only, and CLV-style metrics only against a sharp consensus. Expected: no Brier gain stand-alone; possible positive stacking weight. Flag clearly on the site as experimental.

**E8. TimesFM-as-input stacking** (≈ 4 h). Hypothesis: TimesFM team-points forecasts (MAE 9.79 vs 10.35) and their spread carry a little information beyond the engineered features (their implied pick is 64.4%). Sketch: predicted margin and its 80% interval width as EQUINE features. Evaluation: vs full model without them. Expected: ≤ 0.001 Brier; keep if the GP variance becomes more informative.

**E9. Referee crews** (≈ 4 h). Data: `gameInfo.officials` (verified back to 2012-13). Sketch: per-referee home-cover residual and foul-rate shrinkage estimates (empirical Bayes over prior seasons) as features; crews are known day-of (fits the post-report run). Evaluation: vs market-only baseline; expected ≈ 0 for sides (Pelechrinis: bias shrinking); possible value for totals.

**E10. JEPA (LeJEPA/SIGReg) encoder over per-game team sequences** (≈ 30–40 h). Preconditions: E1–E4 done and TabPFN v2 / CatBoost on the 25 features run as the deep-learning control. Design and verdict in Section 2 Q5. Expected ≤ 0.001 Brier vs stacked baseline; value is in GP-head calibration/OOD, evaluated by ECE and the accuracy-by-novelty-tercile curve.

Appendix experiment (any hosted LLM / Jev as a forecaster): shadow log only, never backtested — protocol in Section 3.

## 5. Phased plan

**Phase 1 — honest ground truth (≈ 25 h, 1–2 weeks of evenings): E3 → E2 → E1.** Start logging today (E3; 3 h). Build the evaluation layer (E2) so every later number is comparable, and read off the open→close gap — the prize size. Then the injury archive (E1), which changes the availability feature for both training and serving. Deliverables: per-game evaluation table, de-vig/consensus module, `data/injury_reports/`, a revised backtest page that shows model vs market-only baseline vs raw price, per season, with CIs.

**Phase 2 — player-level information (≈ 30 h): E4 → E5.** RAPM + projected minutes on top of E1; schedule/circadian features. Decision gate: promote to the site only if the stacked model beats the market-only baseline on pooled 2019-20 → 2025-26 with the CI excluding zero, or beats it vs the open on 2023-26.

**Phase 3 — text and representation (≈ 40–50 h): E6 (S1 → S2) → E7 → E8.** The System One encoder on official-report text first, then the decorrelation variant, then TimesFM stacking. Gate as above; S2 only on 2024-25+ seasons.

**Phase 4 — optional (≈ 35–45 h): E9, E10, and a hosted-LLM shadow log.** Only if Phases 1–3 leave budget; expected value is low and the JEPA work is primarily of UQ-methodological interest (SIGReg embeddings feeding an RFF GP).

Compute check for the daily run: E1 (one PDF fetch + parse) < 10 s; E4 (stint update for ~15 games + ridge refit) < 1 min; E6 inference (15 games × ~1.2k tokens on a 1B int8 model) 3–10 min; everything else is seconds. Total stays well under 15 min on the 4-vCPU runner; the heavy work (historical embedding extraction, LoRA) happens once on the M1 overnight.

### 6.2 Official NBA injury reports (live and Wayback)

- **Live index:** `https://official.nba.com/nba-injury-report-2026-27-season/` (current season only; `…-2025-26-season/`, `…-2024-25-season/`, …, `…-2018-19-season/` all 301 → the current page). The page links PDFs by absolute URL.
- **PDF URL pattern:** `https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/referee/injury/Injury-Report_YYYY-MM-DD_HH_MMAM.pdf` (e.g. `Injury-Report_2026-10-27_12_30AM.pdf`; 15-minute cadence on game days). Older filenames (2018-19) use `_HHAM.pdf` / `_HHPM.pdf` without minutes (e.g. `Injury-Report_2018-12-21_05PM.pdf`). Requests need browser-like headers (`User-Agent: Mozilla/5.0 (Macintosh; …) Chrome/130.0 Safari/537.36`, `Accept: application/pdf,*/*`, `Referer: https://official.nba.com/`). Retention on the CDN: 2026-10-27, 2026-10-22, 2026-04-10, 2026-01-15 → 200 (88–100 KB); 2025-11-20, 2025-03-15, 2024-01-04, 2023-11-20, 2022-03-15, 2020-02-10 → 403 (≈ current season + ~9 months).
- **Wayback enumeration (the query used):**

```
https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=ak-static.cms.nba.com/referee/injury/*&output=json&filter=statuscode:200&fl=original,timestamp&collapse=urlkey
```

  Returns a JSON array whose first row is the header `["original","timestamp"]` followed by one row per unique archived URL (`collapse=urlkey` keeps one capture per URL; `filter=statuscode:200` drops redirects/errors). Result on 2026-10-27: **14,536 unique PDFs** (without the status filter the agents counted 14,596; `showNumPages=true` reports 4 pages for the default paging, but the single call above returned everything in ~60 s). Per-season count of distinct game-days with ≥1 archived PDF (season = calendar year of the date if month ≥ 10, else year − 1): 2018-19: 104, 2019-20: 205, 2020-21: 206, 2021-22: 243, 2022-23: 227, 2023-24: 224, 2024-25: 228, 2025-26: 220, 2026-27: 1 (so far). The `availability` API (`https://archive.org/wayback/available?url=…`) returns empty for individual PDFs and should not be used; use the CDX listing.
- **Fetching an archived PDF:** `https://web.archive.org/web/{timestamp}id_/{original}` (the `id_` flag returns the raw file without the Wayback banner), e.g. `https://web.archive.org/web/20231002183728id_/https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/referee/injury/Injury-Report_2018-12-21_05PM.pdf`. Rate-limit (≈1 request/s) and cache locally; total ≈ 0.4 GB.
- **Publication time from the filename:** regex `Injury-Report_(\d{4})-(\d{2})-(\d{2})_(\d{2})(?:_(\d{2}))?(AM|PM)\.pdf` (case-insensitive) → date, hour (12-hour), optional minutes, meridian; the time is **Eastern Time** (the PDF header repeats it as "Injury Report: 12/21/18 05:30 PM"). Convert to UTC with `America/New_York`. The Wayback capture `timestamp` is irrelevant for alignment — only the filename time is the publication time.
- **PDF table layout (parsed with `pypdf` `extract_text()` in layout mode; `pdfplumber` also works):**
  - 2018-12-21 (earliest archived): columns `Game Date | Game Time | Matchup | Team | Player Name | Category | Reason | Current Status | Previous Status` — e.g. `Henson, John | Injury/Illness | Left Wrist Surgery | Out | -`; `Jones, Jalen | G League Team | - | Out | -`.
  - 2019-11-15: `Category` and `Reason` merged into one `Reason` column (`Injury/Illness - Right Knee Soreness`, `G League - Two-Way`, `G League - On Assignment`); `Previous Status` still present.
  - 2020-01-20 onward: columns `Game Date | Game Time | Matchup | Team | Player Name | Current Status | Reason`, with the standardized reason format `Injury/Illness - <Body part>; <Detail>` (e.g. `Injury/Illness - Left Knee; Surgery`), plus non-injury reasons `G League - Two-Way`, `G League - On Assignment`, `Not With Team`, `Health and Safety Protocols` (2020-22), `Return to Competition Reconditioning`, `Coach's Decision - …`, `Injury/Illness - N/a; Surgical Recovery`; teams that have not filed appear as `NOT YET SUBMITTED`.
  - **Reason-column start date: 21 Dec 2018 (the first archived report); the structured "Body part; Detail" form starts January 2020.** Statuses: Out, Doubtful, Questionable, Probable, Available. Game Date/Time/Matchup cells are blank on continuation rows and must be forward-filled; multi-line reasons wrap onto the following line.
